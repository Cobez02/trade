# PREREG 3A-10 — Overnight drift in Treasury futures (ZN, ZB) and gold (GC): is the night premium equity-only?
Written 2026-10-14 BEFORE buying the data. Purchase approved by Connor (ZN $15.83 + ZB $14.73 + GC $17.14 = $47.70; spent
before this test $58.18; after it ~$105.88 of $125). The loader's budget check still governs.

- Mechanism (who pays, why): the equity overnight premium (Lou-Polk-Skouras; Kelly-Clark) is compensation for carrying equity
  gap risk while cash is shut. Treasuries are the hedge asset: the same stress that pays the equity holder overnight pays the
  Treasury SHORT, so the structural prediction is NO positive drift in ZN/ZB (if anything, slightly negative long). Gold trades
  physical demand in Asian hours (inside our window) and is sold into the London AM fix (05:30 CT, also inside): documented
  positive overnight / negative intraday pattern in gold (Caminschi & Heaney 2014 on the fix; "gold is bought in Asia and sold in
  London"), so a small positive drift is plausible. This is the test of whether the night premium is an equity phenomenon.
- Source: Lucca-Moench 2015 find no pre-FOMC drift in Treasuries; Jones, Lamont, Lumsdaine 1998 (Treasury returns concentrate on
  announcement days, not overnight); Caminschi & Heaney 2014 (gold fix).
- Venue constraints: prop single-session (Topstep allows ZN/ZB/GC). Full-size contracts (no micro for ZN/ZB; MGC exists for gold
  but PROGRAM.md's cost table is full-size GC — used as written).
- Data: Databento GLBX.MDP3 ohlcv-1m, ZN.v.0, ZB.v.0, GC.v.0, 2010-06-06..2023-12-31. Holdout untouched. Identical window to the
  equity tests (17:02 -> 08:30 CT) so the comparison is like-for-like; this is not the bond/gold pit-session boundary.
- Rule, exactly: buy 1 contract at the 17:02 open, 1% stop (of price; on ZN that is ~1.25 pts = $1,250, so it almost never
  binds), sell at the 08:30 open. Sun-Thu. Costs per PROGRAM.md: ZN $2.00 + 1 tick ($15.625) per side = $35.25 RT; ZB $2.00 +
  $31.25 = $66.50 RT; GC $2.50 + $10.00 = $25.00 RT. Points: ZN/ZB $1,000/pt, GC $100/pt.
- Grid (PBO only): stop in {0, 0.75%, 1%, 1.5%, 2%}, as 3A-1; headline 1%.
- Predictions (gross/night vs each contract's round trip):
  * ZN: -1 to +1 bp/night = -$12 to +$12 gross per night vs $35.25 RT (< 0.4x); FAIL by a wide margin; Sharpe -0.3 to +0.2.
  * ZB: -1 to +2 bp = -$15 to +$30 vs $66.50 RT (< 0.5x); FAIL. Worst night -$2,000 to -$4,000 (2020-03, 2022).
  * GC: 0 to +3 bp = $0 to +$45 vs $25.00 RT; the only one with a chance at the ratio (> 1x needs > 1.7 bp). PF 0.95-1.15,
    cost share 60% or more. I expect a weak positive drift, insufficient for the gate: FAIL on DSR and cost share.
  * Trades/yr ~245 each. Equity-only verdict if ZN and ZB show <= 0 bp and GC < 2 bp.
- Pass criteria (PROGRAM.md §1 gate per contract): DSR p >= 0.95, PF >= 1.15, cost share < 40%, worst night inside the venue floor

- What would make me distrust a pass: the .v.0 roll inside the window (ZN/ZB roll quarterly with a carry-driven basis jump:
  quantify roll nights); a GC pass driven by 2010-11 or 2019-20 alone; any bp estimate whose sign flips with the stop grid.

- Predictions: ZN -1..+1 bp predicted, -0.1; ZB -1..+2, -0.1; GC 0..+3 predicted, -1.0 realised (below range). Verdict as predicted.
Equity-only verdict: the Globex night premium is an index-futures phenomenon. Treasuries carry nothing overnight (consistent with
their role as the hedge asset); gold is slightly negative. 3A-10 is retired; ZN/ZB/GC are not used again in Phase 1 unless a
test has a different mechanism (3A-11 CL inventory-day is event-driven and is a separate question).
